### 7.2. Formative Testing Key Outcomes

* **Task 1 (One-Handed Navigation):**
  * **Selected Variant:** `Nav-1: Fixed Bottom Navigation Bar` (Rating: **9.3/10**).
  * **Evaluation:** Achieved the fastest completion time (**1.2s**) with **1 tap**. All tabs sit within the natural thumb sweep zone. In contrast, `Nav-2` (FAB Menu) required 2 taps and occluded content, while `Nav-3` (Bottom Hamburger) created unnecessary modal overhead.
* **Task 2 (Match Schedule & Tournament Details):**
  * **Selected Variant:** `Sch-2: Date Strip + Match Detail` (Rating: **9.0/10**).
  * **Evaluation:** The horizontal date carousel provides rapid day-by-day browsing familiar to sports fans. The clear information hierarchy (Tournament → Date → Match Cards → Match Detail Screen) outperformed `Sch-1` (Accordion with long vertical scroll) and `Sch-3` (Timeline requiring continuous dragging).
* **Actionable Improvement Points for PA4 (Hi-fi Prototype):**
  1. Add persistent text labels beneath Bottom Nav icons to eliminate iconography ambiguity.
  2. Implement quick filter chips on Schedule (`Live`, `Upcoming`, `Finished`).
  3. Ensure minimum 44×44px touch targets for `Detail ->` action buttons.

### 8.1. What Went Well
* **High-Quality Paper Prototypes:** The hand-drawn prototypes using colored markers on unlined paper were crisp, visually appealing, and highly legible. Interactive cutout overlays (dropdowns, menus) worked smoothly with the physical paper phone case.
* **Rigorous Testing Protocol:** Strict adherence to the *Think-Aloud Protocol* with distinct roles (Facilitator, Computer, Observer) allowed the team to uncover unexpected user insights without biasing participants.
* **Interactive Presentation Innovation:** Designing a self-contained Swiss Modern HTML/CSS slide deck with an embedded smartphone simulator elevated the presentation quality significantly above standard static slides.

### 8.2. What Went Wrong & Problems Encountered
* **"Computer" Operator Latency:** In initial test runs of Task 2 (multi-state schedule scrolling), swapping paper sheets manually had slight delays compared to rapid user tapping reflexes.
  * *Root Cause:* Insufficient rehearsal time for complex multi-layer paper swaps before the first test session.
* **Participant Scheduling Friction:** Coordinating schedules with external participants took longer than anticipated due to university class conflicts.
  * *Root Cause:* Booking test slots only 2 days in advance instead of a week earlier.

### 8.3. What Can Be Done Differently in Next Sprint (PA4)
* **Figma Design System Setup Early:** Establish design tokens (colors, typography, spacing, components) in Figma during Week 1 of PA4 to streamline Hi-fi prototyping.
* **Summative Usability Testing Protocol:** Schedule 5+ external participants at the start of Sprint 4 and implement standardized usability metrics (SUS questionnaire, Single Ease Question, task completion rates).

### 8.4. Key Lessons Learned
* Paper prototyping is exceptionally cost-effective: identifying the flaws in the radial FAB menu and timeline scroll on paper saved dozens of hours of coding/hi-fi design time.
* Having a dedicated "Observer/Note-taker" separate from the "Facilitator" is vital for capturing verbatim user quotes and subtle micro-hesitations.
